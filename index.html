Research vision

How does experience become lasting skill?

Our clearest molecules-to-behavior accounts of learning come from simple behaviors that can be traced to identified synapses. My goal is to bring that same mechanistic resolution to complex, culturally transmitted skills such as speech and music.

Birdsong makes this possible. Juvenile songbirds learn complex vocal sequences through imitation and prolonged practice, using a dedicated cortico-basal ganglia circuit that sharply constrains where to look for the mechanisms of learning.

Ironically, birdsong’s greatest strengths have also been its biggest experimental barriers: the sheer abundance (~100,000 renditions) and complexity of song makes learning difficult to quantify, while the dedicated neural circuitry is difficult to target with tools developed primarily for mammals. I overcame both barriers by combining AI-based quantification of song learning with optogenetic, chemogenetic, and biosensor tools adapted to the songbird, gaining direct access to the circuits and signaling pathways that drive learning.

Selected discoveries

Nature · 2025

Dopamine tracks vocal performance and instructs learning

We showed that dopamine in the song basal ganglia tracks the quality of individual vocal renditions and is required for juvenile song learning, revealing how the brain evaluates performance during natural, self-guided learning.

Qi et al., Nature (2025) →

Nature · 2026

A synaptic locus of song learning

I identified the specific cortico-basal ganglia synapses required to express recently acquired vocal changes and showed that these changes depend on these synapses for only a few hours before consolidating downstream. In the same study, increasing basal ganglia activity accelerated learning at a cost to copying fidelity.

Schreiner et al., Nature (2026) →

Future lab

These discoveries provide a synaptic foothold for understanding how a complex, culturally transmitted skill is learned. My independent lab will use this foothold to build a molecules-to-behavior account of complex skill learning through three linked questions:

01 · Acquisition

How does delayed evaluation modify the correct synapses?

I will determine how delayed evaluation is linked to the neural activity that produced a successful action, revealing how moment-to-moment experience drives lasting changes in behavior.

02 · Consolidation

How are newly acquired changes stabilized into lasting skill?

I will determine where and how rapid learning is transferred into downstream motor circuits.

03 · Optimization

What sets the speed and fidelity of learning?

I will determine how biological constraints tune the balance between learning speed, copying fidelity, and behavioral variation.

Together, these projects will identify the molecular, cirucit, and computational mechanisms that allow one animal to imitate the behavior of another - a core capacity for cultural transmission across individuals and generations.
